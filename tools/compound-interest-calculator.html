What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and the interest accumulated over previous periods. It allows savings and investments to grow through compounding.

Compound Interest Formula

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

A = Final amount
P = Initial principal
r = Annual interest rate as a decimal
n = Number of compounding periods per year
t = Time in years

Example of Compound Interest

Suppose you invest ₹10,000 at an annual interest rate of 10%, compounded annually, for 2 years.

After 2 years, the total amount will be ₹12,100, giving you ₹2,100 in interest.

Compound Interest Calculation Examples

Example 1: Annual Compounding

Suppose you invest ₹10,000 at an annual interest rate of 10% for 2 years.

  • Initial investment: ₹10,000
  • Annual interest rate: 10%
  • Investment period: 2 years
  • Compounding frequency: Annually
  • Final amount: ₹12,100
  • Total interest: ₹2,100

Example 2: Understanding Compounding

In the first year, ₹10,000 earns ₹1,000 in interest. In the second year, interest is calculated on ₹11,000, not just the original ₹10,000.

Why Compound Interest Matters

Compound interest is calculated on both the original principal and accumulated interest. Over time, this can increase the value of an investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is compound interest different from simple interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal and accumulated interest.

Does compounding frequency matter?

Yes. With the same annual rate and duration, more frequent compounding generally produces a higher final amount.

Can compound interest be negative?

Compound interest can be negative when the interest rate is negative, although ordinary savings calculations generally use non-negative rates.
